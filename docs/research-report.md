# Workforce Scheduling & Shift Optimization Platform: Research Report

> **Where the code is.** The experiment scripts that produced these results are part of the source package, which is distributed separately (see [SOURCE-ACCESS.md](../SOURCE-ACCESS.md)). The raw results they wrote are included in this repository under [`results/`](../results/).

> **Status: protocol v0.2, results recorded (2026-10-21).** Hypotheses, methods and the statistical plan were fixed in v0.1, before the experiments ran; v0.2 adds the results and logs every deviation in the [change log](#12-change-log). **Every instance and demand series in this report is synthetic (generated by `wfs.generator` / `wfs.demand`); nothing was measured on a real workplace.** All numbers below are copied from `results/summary.md`, which `experiments/summarize.py` generates from the raw per-run files in `results/*_runs.csv`.

## Abstract

This study asks: how do exact constraint-programming and simple heuristics compare on schedule quality, fairness and solve time across problem sizes, and how much does forecast quality change staffing outcomes? On generated instances, with at most 2 solver workers on a shared, heavily loaded machine and time limits of 20 to 60 seconds: (H1) CP-SAT removed most of the overtime cost of a greedy heuristic on instances of 100 or more employees (median relative reduction 100%, 95% CI 87% to 100%, n = 20 paired instances) while lowering total labour cost by only about 0.4% to 1.1%; (H2) a fairness weight cut the Gini coefficient of undesirable shifts by 55% to 60% at weights of 100 to 300 for a 1.1% to 1.8% median cost increase, but with more unfilled slots, more disliked assignments and more overtime within the time limit; (H3) the time to first solution grew super-linearly (log-log slope 1.29, CI 1.16 to 1.39), and decomposition by team stayed within the time budget but was not better than a single model below 500 employees, and a greedy heuristic beat every CP-SAT variant at 1,000 employees; (H4) 10% lower injected forecast error gave a statistically detectable but small reduction in understaffed requirement lines (0.3 to 0.9 lines out of 62, 4% to 7%). The conclusions are limited to the generator used and should not be read as predictions for real sites.

## 1. Research question

How do exact constraint-programming and simple heuristics compare on schedule quality, fairness and solve time across problem sizes, and how much does forecast quality change staffing outcomes?

## 2. Hypotheses

- H1: CP-SAT schedules reduce overtime cost by at least 10% versus a greedy heuristic on instances of 100 or more employees, within a five-minute time limit.
- H2: Adding an explicit fairness objective reduces the Gini coefficient of undesirable shifts by at least 30% at under 3% cost increase.
- H3: Solve time grows super-linearly with employees and skills, and a decomposition by team keeps large instances within the time limit.
- H4: A 10% improvement in forecast error produces a measurable reduction in understaffed intervals.

## 3. Background

- Staff scheduling and rostering has a long literature; Ernst et al. (2004) review applications, methods and models, and Burke et al. (2004) survey nurse rostering, the best-studied case, where metaheuristics and exact methods both appear.
- Constraint programming with a SAT-based engine (OR-Tools CP-SAT, Perron & Furnon) is a common modern choice for rostering because rules map directly onto constraints; its optimality gap can stay large on cost objectives with weak linear bounds, which this study observes.
- Public rostering benchmark sets (for example Curtois & Qu, 2014) exist; this study did not run them (different instance format), which limits external validity.
- For demand forecasting the baselines are seasonal-naive and exponential smoothing (Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, 2021); gradient boosting (Friedman, 2001) is the machine-learning comparison.
- Fairness in rostering has several definitions; this study fixes one (Gini coefficient of a burden score) before running.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 System under test

A scheduling platform for shift-based operations such as warehouses, contact centers, hospitals and retail chains. A constraint solver builds schedules that respect labor rules, skills and preferences, forecasts drive the staffing targets, and managers keep control through what-if scenarios and manual overrides. The implementation is in this repository (Python 3.12, OR-Tools CP-SAT, scikit-learn, statsmodels, FastAPI, SQLite).

Objective used in every experiment (integers; cost in half-currency units so a 50% overtime premium stays integral): 20,000 per missing person-slot (soft coverage) + labour cost (regular hour 2 x rate, overtime hour 3 x rate; overtime is hours beyond an employee's weekly contract hours) + 40 per dislike point + lambda x (sum over employees of the absolute deviation of the undesirable-shift burden from the median burden). Hard constraints: availability, time off, skills and certifications, no overlap, contractual weekly maximum, at most 12 h per day and 48 h per week, 11 h rest, at most 6 consecutive days (the "medium" rule set). Burden points: night shift 2, plus 1 on weekend days.

### 4.2 Environment

Instances come from `wfs.generator.generate_instance` (seeded, documented in the module docstring): one site, three shift types (early, late, night; whole-hour shifts), employees with skills (general, senior, specialist), a first-aid certification, contracts of 24, 32 or 40 contract hours, random availability, time off and preferences, and synthetic demand with weekly seasonality, trend, known promotional events and lognormal noise (`wfs.demand`). Required person-shifts are scaled to a utilisation of the contracted capacity (0.80 unless stated). The rule density parameter (low, medium, high) exists in the generator but was not varied.

Fixed settings: OR-Tools 9.15.6755, `num_workers = 2` (never more), `random_seed = 1`, relative gap limit 1% (2% in E4), time limits per experiment below. Hardware: AMD EPYC 7543P (4 physical / 8 logical cores as reported by the OS), 28 GB RAM, Windows 11, Python 3.12.13, scikit-learn 1.9.1, statsmodels 0.15.0, numpy 2.5.4 (also stored in `results/*_meta.json`). **The machine was shared with other jobs: the system CPU load measured just before each run had a median of 98% to 100% across experiments** (`cpu_load_pct` column), so wall times and time-limited objectives are noisy and probably pessimistic.

### 4.3 Baselines

- Greedy constructive heuristic (`wfs.solver.greedy`): chronological, cheapest marginal cost (including the overtime premium) among feasible employees, using the same rule validators as the exact model.
- CP-SAT without fairness objective: the lambda = 0 condition of E2.
- "Last period's schedule repeated" (listed in v0.1): **not implemented**; see the change log.
- Additional arms not in v0.1: CP-SAT warm-started from the greedy schedule (E1, E3), decomposition by team (E3).

### 4.4 Independent variables

- Solver method
- Instance size
- Fairness weight
- Forecast error level
- Rule density (implemented in the generator, not varied in E1 to E4)

### 4.5 Metrics

- Overtime cost and total labor cost
- Coverage (understaffed intervals). Operationalised as *requirement lines*: one line is one (shift, role, optional certification) staffing requirement; a line is understaffed if fewer people than required are assigned. Missing person-slots are also reported.
- Gini coefficient of undesirable shifts (over employees who could work at least one undesirable shift)
- Preference satisfaction rate (share of assignments that are not disliked; dislike points are reported in E2)
- Solve time and optimality gap (as reported by the solver; for time-limited runs the gap is often large because the lower bound is weak, even when the solution is good)

## 6. Statistical plan

- **Replication:** each condition is run enough times to estimate variance; the run count is fixed before data collection and reported. *Achieved:* 5 (E1), 8 (E2), 3 (E3), 20 (E4, F0) independent instances per condition. Each instance was solved once per arm (one solver seed), so within-instance solver variance under contention is not separately estimated.
- **Summaries:** medians (or means where appropriate) with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, plus the spread (IQR or standard deviation). *Achieved:* medians and percentile bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples of instances); spread is visible in the raw CSVs.
- **Comparisons:** paired, non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank or Mann–Whitney U) unless distributional assumptions are checked and hold. *Achieved:* paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank throughout.
- **Effect sizes:** reported alongside p-values (Cliff’s delta or Cohen’s d); a significant but trivial effect is called trivial.
- **Multiple comparisons:** Holm–Bonferroni correction across the hypotheses in each experiment. *Achieved:* Holm within each family of paired comparisons shown in a table; not across experiments.
- **No silent exclusions:** outliers are flagged and reported, and any removal needs a written, pre-stated rule. *Achieved:* no run was excluded. Relative overtime reductions are undefined when greedy has no overtime; those instances are counted separately ("pairs with greedy OT>0").
- **Negative results are results:** if a hypothesis is not supported, the report says so.

## 8. Discussion

**H1 (supported on this generator, with a changed time limit).** CP-SAT eliminated almost all overtime that the greedy heuristic created (median relative reduction 100%, CI 87% to 100%, 20 paired instances of 100 and 200 employees; large effect). That clears the 10% bar by a wide margin, but the *total* labour cost differed by only about 0.4% to 1.1%, because overtime premium is a small part of cost; a reader who cares about total cost should not expect much more. The greedy baseline is deliberately cost-aware (it prices the overtime premium), which makes it a fairly strong baseline; a cost-blind heuristic was not tested. The tested limit was 60 s, not the pre-registered five minutes (change log). The picture is not uniformly positive for the solver: at 1,000 employees no CP-SAT variant found a schedule with full coverage in 60 s, and the greedy heuristic did (E3), so "CP-SAT beats greedy" is a statement about 100 to 200 employees within 60 s.

**H2 (supported on the stated criteria, with real costs on other axes).** A fairness weight of 100 to 300 reduced the Gini coefficient by about 55% to 60% for a 1.1% to 1.8% median cost increase, inside the "30% for under 3%" criterion; the effect on Gini was significant at every weight tried (Holm p = 0.047, n = 8). Three caveats weaken the headline. (i) Fairness was bought partly with other things: dislike points roughly tripled (32 to 98 at lambda = 300), overtime rose from 4 to 15 hours on average, and unfilled person-slots rose from 2.4 to 3.4; part of the shortfall growth is the 30 s time limit, because high weights make the search harder (median gap 0.16 to 0.63), not necessarily a true cost of fairness. (ii) The frontier is not monotone (Gini at lambda = 30 is above lambda = 10, and at 3000 above 1000), which is what noisy time-limited solves would produce. (iii) The Gini of small integer burdens (a few night shifts per person over 14 days) is a blunt measure and the baseline instances already contain unavoidable shortfall.

**H3 (partly supported; the decomposition half is weak).** Time to first solution grew faster than linearly (slope 1.29, CI 1.16 to 1.39; 1.41 with a warm start) and model build time roughly linearly (1.10, CI 1.00 to 1.19), so super-linear growth is supported for time to a first solution, while time to optimality cannot be estimated because almost no run proved optimality. Decomposition by team did stay within the time budget in 18 of 18 runs, but so did the monolithic model (it simply stops at the limit), so that criterion is not informative. On quality, decomposition was worse than the single model at 100 and 200 employees (objective ratios 1.32 and 1.54 against 1.00 and 1.001), better than the cold-started single model at 500 and 1,000 employees (shortfall 19 against 36, and 93 against 486), worse than the warm-started single model at 500 (shortfall 19 against 0), and never better than the greedy heuristic from 100 employees up (median greedy shortfall 0 from 50 employees up). A single model warm-started from the greedy schedule was the best CP-SAT variant at 500 employees (full coverage, gap 0.016), but at 1,000 employees every CP-SAT variant left hundreds of slots unfilled (shortfall 93 decomposed, 362 warm-started, 486 cold). The decomposition tested is a simple proportional split of demand across teams; it says little about better decompositions. The practical guidance from this data is to warm-start CP-SAT from a fast heuristic and to treat "which method" as size-dependent.

**H4 (supported as "measurable", but small).** Across 20 cases, a 10% lower injected error produced a statistically detectable reduction in understaffed lines (Holm p between 0.008 and 0.034), but the size was 0.3 to 0.9 lines out of 62 (4% to 7% of the mean), with negligible-to-small Cliff's delta. Over the whole range the dose-response is 0.45 additional understaffed lines per percentage point of MAPE (CI 0.35 to 0.56). In the model design, ETS (WAPE 0.078 in E4, 0.083 in F0) and gradient boosting (0.085 / 0.082) were statistically indistinguishable and both beat seasonal-naive on WAPE; ETS's advantage over seasonal-naive carried through to fewer understaffed lines (Holm p = 0.0086), while gradient boosting's did not reach significance. Gradient boosting did not outperform ETS despite being the only model that saw the event calendar. Also surprising: understaffing at sigma = 0.05 is already 3.5 lines, because the solver meets the forecast plan exactly and any under-forecast of a small team becomes a gap; slack (the safety factor in the app) was not tested.

**Practical guidance.** (1) Use the solver for overtime and rule compliance, but warm-start it and keep a fast heuristic for very large instances. (2) Pick the fairness weight from a sweep on the site's own data; expect to pay in preference satisfaction and overtime as well as in cost. (3) Invest in forecast quality, but expect gradual returns on understaffing; staffing buffers and swap/open-shift processes may matter more. (4) None of this has been tested on real data.

## 9. Threats to validity

- Generated instances may be easier or harder than real sites: use public benchmarks and client data when available. Here nothing real was used; the instance family, its distributions and the staffing policy were written by the author and their parameters (for example 25% of employees refuse nights, 15% contracts of 24 hours) were set before the full runs but not validated against any workplace.
- Solver parameters influence results: settings fixed and published (2 workers, default CP-SAT search, seed 1, gap limit 1% or 2%). Default parameters were not tuned.
- **Shared machine and contention.** Three projects ran on the same 8 logical cores while these experiments ran; the system CPU load just before runs had a median of about 98% to 100%. Time-limited CP-SAT results (objective at the limit, gap, time to first solution) are non-deterministic across runs and probably pessimistic. Each (instance, arm) was run once with a single solver seed, so solver run-to-run variance is folded into the between-instance spread rather than estimated separately.
- **Time limits shorter than pre-registered.** H1 and H3 named a five-minute limit; 20 to 60 s were used because of the shared machine. Results at five minutes could differ, especially for the large-instance arms.
- Fairness has competing definitions: metrics chosen before running experiments. Gini on a burden score was fixed; the solver optimises a different surrogate (absolute deviation from the median), so a lower surrogate does not guarantee a lower Gini.
- Weak lower bounds: reported optimality gaps are large even for good solutions (median 0.16 to 0.63 in E2), so the gap says little about solution quality. Objective values are therefore compared across arms rather than against bounds.
- Confounding with unavoidable shortfall: some instances cannot be fully covered (E2 baseline mean shortfall 2.4 person-slots; E4 oracle up to 11), and shortfall is weighted 20,000 per slot; cost comparisons across arms with different shortfalls are therefore reported with shortfall alongside, and the cost increase in E2 is also given for no-shortfall pairs.
- Small samples: 3 instances per size in E3 and 8 in E2 give wide intervals; Holm correction is within, not across, experiments; many cells of the E1 table have 5 instances.
- Deviations were made after seeing partial results in two places (the E3 greedy arm; the E2 paired tests, which were in the plan but added to the summary script after the E2 data existed); see the change log. Analysis code was otherwise fixed before the full run.
- The forecasters were evaluated on synthetic demand that the generator draws from a simple seasonal-plus-event model with lognormal noise. ETS and gradient boosting are nearly matched to that structure; real demand is likely harder.
- Researcher degrees of freedom: analysis choices are fixed in this protocol; any deviation is logged in the change log below.
